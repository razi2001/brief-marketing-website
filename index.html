HOW IT WORKS

Three steps. The boring part disappears.

Brief captures what you mean in the moment. Your coding agent — the one already sitting in your repo — turns it into a proper ticket, with your code's context baked in.

01

Capture it

Hit the toolbar and talk through the bug while it's on screen — or take a screenshot and draw on it, or just type a line. Whatever's fastest.

02

It queues locally

Each brief saves to a folder on your machine and stacks up in the popup. Capture as many as you want through the day — no accounts, no upload.

03

Your agent files it

Hit Export, paste the prompt into your coding agent. It reads each brief, writes a clean ticket in your tracker, groups duplicates, and cleans up after itself.

WHY BRIEF

Say no to the contextless wall of text.

You know the ticket a generic chatbot spits out: padded, vague, hedged, and somehow three paragraphs long — written by something that never saw your screen or your code. A brief gives your agent the actual moment and your repo, so the ticket comes out sharp.

FAQ

Questions, briefly answered.

What do I need for it to work?
Chrome (or any Chromium browser — Edge, Brave, Arc) and a coding agent with file access and a tracker connected — Claude Code, Cursor, or similar with a Linear / Jira / GitHub / Notion integration. Brief does the capturing; your agent does the filing.
Does my voice or screen get uploaded anywhere?
No. Brief is local-first. Every capture is written to ~/Downloads/brief/ on your own machine. Nothing is sent to a server. The only thing that ever leaves is what your own agent reads when you explicitly export and hand it the briefs.
Why is the ticket better than one from a hosted tool?
Because your own agent files it from inside your repo, it has your codebase as context. It can point at the actual component, reference the real function, and match your team's ticket conventions — things a server that's never seen your code simply can't do.
Do I have to record audio every time?
Not at all. A brief can be a screen + voice recording, an annotated screenshot, a typed description, or a mix. Use whatever's fastest for the thing you're reporting.
What happens to a brief after it's filed?
Once your agent has filed the ticket, it deletes that brief's file from the folder — so your capture folder stays clean and nothing lingers. Duplicates across briefs get grouped into one ticket.
Which trackers are supported?
Any that your coding agent can reach. Linear, Jira, GitHub Issues, and Notion are the common ones — Brief just hands your agent the brief and a short instruction; the agent files it wherever you've connected it.
How much does it cost? Is it really open source?
Yes — the extension is free and open source (MIT), and the code is on GitHub. You bring your own coding agent, so there's no per-ticket cost from us. A cloud-hosted version — which files tickets for you, no agent setup needed — is coming for people who'd rather not run their own.
